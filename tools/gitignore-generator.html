Why this matters

Git tracks every file you add, and once a secret is committed it stays in history even after you delete it. A good .gitignore prevents the mistake up front. The Env & secrets block is selected by default because that is where the real risk lives — .env files, private keys and service-account JSON.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should always be in a .gitignore?

At minimum: your .env and .env.* files, private keys (*.pem, *.key), service-account JSON, and your dependency and build folders (node_modules, vendor, dist). Keep a committed .env.example with placeholder values instead.

Does .gitignore remove a file already committed to Git?

No. .gitignore only stops new files from being tracked. A secret already in history must be rotated and scrubbed from history with a tool like git filter-repo or BFG.
