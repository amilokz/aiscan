A handful of HTTP headers block clickjacking, MIME sniffing, and forced-HTTP downgrade attacks. Copy a recommended set for your server below.

What these headers do

  • Content-Security-Policy — limits where scripts and styles can load from, the main defense against XSS.
  • Strict-Transport-Security — forces HTTPS so attackers cannot downgrade the connection.
  • X-Frame-Options — blocks your site being framed for clickjacking.
  • X-Content-Type-Options — stops browsers from MIME-sniffing responses.
  • Referrer-Policy & Permissions-Policy — limit what you leak and which device APIs pages can use.

The CSP here is a safe starting point; tighten it to match the domains your app actually loads from.

FAQ

What are the most important security headers?

Content-Security-Policy and Strict-Transport-Security do the most work, followed by X-Frame-Options, X-Content-Type-Options, Referrer-Policy and Permissions-Policy.

Will a Content-Security-Policy break my site?

A strict CSP can block inline scripts or third-party resources. Start from the policy here, test, and add the specific sources your app needs rather than loosening it to allow everything.
